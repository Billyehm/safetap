# What your phone can do: Android vs iPhone

SafeTap works on both, but the two systems give apps different powers. Here is exactly what changes depending on the phone in your hand. No fine print.

## The SOS button

**Android:** The home-screen widget has a real button. Press and hold it for 3 seconds, and the SOS fires right from the home screen. You never open the app.

**iPhone (iOS 17 and newer):** Same idea, adapted. Tap the red SOS widget and a 3-second arming window starts, then a 5-second countdown, then the SOS fires. A Cancel button stays on screen the whole time, so a pocket tap never sends an alert by itself.

**iPhone (iOS 16):** Apple only allowed tappable widget buttons from iOS 17. On iOS 16, tapping the widget opens the SafeTap app to a confirmation screen, and one more tap sends the SOS. Same result, one extra step.

## Lock screen

**Android:** The widget can sit on the home screen and, on supported phones, the lock screen.

**iPhone:** Add SafeTap to your Lock Screen as a compact SOS slot (iOS 16 and newer). It behaves like the home-screen widget: direct fire on iOS 17+, deep link into the app on iOS 16.

## Text messages to your contacts

**Android:** The phone can send the SMS itself, quietly, the moment you trigger the SOS. Your contacts get the text even if our servers are slow.

**iPhone:** Apple does not let any app send texts silently in the background, and apps that try get rejected from the App Store. So on iPhone the SMS goes out from SafeTap's servers instead. Your contacts still get the text, the call, and the WhatsApp message with your live tracking link, and nobody needs the app installed to receive any of it.

If you ever want to text from your own number, the app opens your Messages app with the contacts and the SOS message already filled in. You just press send.

## Live location sharing

**Both:** While an SOS is active, your location is shared with your contacts every 15 seconds through the tracking link.

**Both:** When there is no active SOS, SafeTap does not track you. No background location, no passive monitoring, on either system.

## False alarm

**Both:** Cancelling an SOS requires your face or fingerprint, so nobody holding your phone can quietly stand down a real emergency.

## The bottom line

On Android the widget is slightly more direct. On iPhone the server sends the texts and iOS 16 needs one extra confirming tap. Everything your contacts receive, the SMS, the call, the WhatsApp message, the live tracking link, the check-in timer, is identical on both.
